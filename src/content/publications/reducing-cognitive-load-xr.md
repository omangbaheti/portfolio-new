abstract: 'We examine cognitive load in room-scale XR workspaces and propose a set of layout heuristics that measurably reduce task-set switching across common spatial workflows.'

## Background

Room-scale workspaces translate 2D windowing habits into 3D space, but the
freedom to place anything anywhere comes at a cost. Users frequently lose track
of **where** an instrument is, and switching between them incurs a measurable
cognitive tax.

## Method

We ran a **between-subjects study (N = 48)** comparing a free-form spatial layout
against a zone-constrained layout derived from our heuristic set. Measures
included task completion time, error rate, and NASA-TLX workload.

| Layout | Completion (s) | Errors | TLX |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Free-form | 184 | 6.2 | 64 |
| Zone-constrained | 141 | 3.1 | 48 |

## Findings

Zone-constrained layouts reduced mean task-completion time by **23%** and
self-reported workload by roughly a quarter.

> Constraint is not the enemy of spatial UX — unmanaged constraint is.

## Implications

We argue that XR toolchain designers should expose explicit *placement zones*
with predictable controllers, rather than implying infinite spatial freedom
without guidance.
